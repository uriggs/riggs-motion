# Cinematic Brand Film Motion

Use this skill for premium brand films, manifesto videos, launch films, motion systems, and narrative kinetic typography that should feel designed rather than like static title cards.

## Core principle

Build a continuous visual narrative. Do not treat every phrase as a centered title that simply fades or scales in. Each scene should feel connected to the next through motion, geometry, timing, or a persistent visual device.

## Continuous motif

Prefer one recurring visual thread that can travel across scenes, such as:

- a thin line with a moving dot or node
- a path that bends, branches, loops, and later resolves
- an underline that becomes a route
- a small circle that grows into a wipe or transition

The motif should carry the viewer from one idea to the next. It may enter from outside the frame, cross behind or beside typography, bend around elements, or transform into a transition device.

## Typography choreography

Vary entrances and exits across scenes.

Good patterns:

- headline slides in from the left with strong horizontal momentum
- words reveal behind a moving mask or passing line
- one phrase enters oversized and partially cropped, then settles
- text exits by continuing its travel offscreen instead of fading in place
- separate words can arrive with a short stagger
- one scene may use slight rotation or diagonal movement if it supports the story

Avoid using the same scale-up animation for every headline.
Avoid repeated center-screen zooms.
Avoid static title-card pacing.

Typography may intentionally overscan the frame during transitions, but the final readable state must fit safely within the composition.

## Graphic storytelling

Translate spoken meaning into simple visual systems instead of displaying explanatory body copy.

Examples:

- confusion: branching or looping paths, competing nodes, overlapping routes
- clarity: tangled paths straighten, dim, disappear, or converge
- momentum: a dot accelerates along a clean route
- decision: multiple paths collapse into one obvious path
- opportunity: a path opens into larger negative space or reaches a confident endpoint

Keep supporting graphics abstract and brand-appropriate. Do not invent stock-footage-like scenes when geometry and typography can tell the story.

## Scene transitions

Transitions should be motivated by something already moving in the scene.

Examples:

- moving dot reaches the frame edge and pulls the next scene in
- line sweeps across the canvas and becomes the next scene's baseline
- circle expands into a full-frame wipe
- oversized letter or shape crosses camera and reveals the next composition
- one network reorganizes into the next graphic

Prefer transform-driven handoffs over unrelated cuts or generic fades.

## Pacing

Use contrast:

- fast travel followed by a confident hold
- short bursts of motion followed by negative space
- one high-energy transition, then a quieter scene
- allow important phrases time to land

For a 15-20 second film, favor 3 major message beats plus a brief end resolve rather than many small scenes.

## Composition

Premium brand work should use strong negative space, asymmetry, and deliberate scale contrast.

- Not every headline should be centered.
- Use left-aligned and edge-driven compositions when appropriate.
- Keep final readable text within safe margins.
- Large cropped typography is useful during motion, but not as the only readable state.
- Use a light background when requested, with restrained brand color accents.

## Audio synchronization

When audio assets are available, motion should appear to create the sound.

- short tick when a headline locks into place
- clean whoosh or sweep as a line travels quickly
- low pulse when a visual system reorganizes
- impact when the final message resolves
- subtle tonal bed can provide continuity under the whole film

Do not attach an effect to every movement. Use sound at structural moments.

## Voiceover-led design

When the user says supporting copy belongs in voiceover, do not display that copy on screen. Show only the key phrase or headline while the graphic system illustrates the spoken explanation.

Build scene timing around the narration. If exact voice timestamps are not available, leave generous holds so the narration can be added later without rushing the animation.

## Originality

Use references to understand pacing, hierarchy, transitions, and motion grammar, not to duplicate another brand's exact artwork, logo treatment, proprietary graphics, or scene-by-scene composition.
